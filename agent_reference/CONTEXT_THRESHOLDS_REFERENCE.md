# Context Threshold Profile-Selection Reference

This document is the **canonical home for the context-threshold profile-selection
mechanics** — the version-specific rules that determine which Context Quality Curve
threshold profile the `context-reporter` hook (and the statusline scripts) apply to
a given agent, keyed off that agent's own exact model ID. CLAUDE.md § Context &
Session Health carries the operational summary and the four severity levels; this
reference carries the full selection prose (Sol/Astra slug rules, provider prefixes,
malformed-boundary examples, the physical-window-vs-profile separation, the
Codex ~919k lane cap, and the `claude-opus-4-8[1m]` example) so the mechanics live
in one place and CLAUDE.md stays lean.

The `context-reporter` hook computes severity on each agent's behalf — agents act
on the reported severity level, never on their own profile inference. This
reference documents *how* that selection is made, for transparency and maintenance.

## Profile Selection

The hook selects among three profiles from each agent's own exact model ID: Claude Fable/Mythos (which now also carries Opus 5.5), GPT Sol/Astra extended-horizon (exact terminal slugs; since 2026-09-29 also GPT-6 Sol and the flagship `-pro` forms, and since 2026-10-12 GPT-6.1 Sol), and the conservative default used by Opus 5 and earlier Opus models, Sonnet, unknown model IDs, every other GPT variant, GLM models, and all other alternative-provider models unless individually validated and registered.

Profile selection is deliberately version-specific and independent of physical context-window mapping. The validated extended-horizon profile recognizes the registered Claude Fable/Mythos identifiers (currently the `fable-5` and `mythos-5` generations) plus one registered Opus identifier, `claude-opus-5-5`. Because the profile arm grants relaxed thresholds, it is boundary-anchored rather than a loose substring: the terminal slug must be exactly `claude-opus-5-5` or `claude-opus-5-5[1m]`, bare or behind one or more provider path prefixes ending in `/` (e.g., `anthropic/claude-opus-5-5`). Near-misses such as `claude-opus-5-5-mini`, `xclaude-opus-5-5`, and any other trailing modifier remain conservative unless separately registered — the same convention the Sol/Astra arm uses. (The *physical-window* arm, by contrast, still uses the loose `*opus-5*`/`*opus-5-5*` substrings, since capacity mapping is the lower-stakes lookup.) Exact GPT 5.6 Sol and GPT-6 Astra share a separate validated profile with independent 60%/75%/90% percentage boundaries and higher validated absolute gates (300k/400k/500k); Astra joins this same extended-horizon profile at Sol parity, and Sol's own tier history is unchanged. For this GPT Sol/Astra profile, the terminal model slug must be exactly `gpt-5.6-sol`, `gpt-6-astra`, (since 2026-09-29, see below) `gpt-6-sol`, or (since 2026-10-12, see below) `gpt-6.1-sol`, optionally followed by `-pro` and then optionally by `[1m]` — the grammar is `^(gpt-5\.6-sol|gpt-6-(astra|sol)|gpt-6\.1-sol)(-pro)?(\[1m\])?$` tested against the provider-stripped terminal slug; the identifier may be bare or may contain one or more provider path prefixes ending in `/`. Malformed left-boundary strings such as `xgpt-5.6-sol`, `foo-gpt-5.6-sol`, and `vendor/notgpt-5.6-sol` remain conservative, as do right-side suffix or trailing variants (including near-misses such as `gpt-6-astra-pro-x`, and `gpt-6-sol[1m]-pro`, where `-pro` follows rather than precedes `[1m]`). GPT is not part of the Claude Fable/Mythos model family. Terra and Luna (including `gpt-6-luna` and their `-pro` forms), mini, chat (including mini/chat `-pro` spellings such as `gpt-5.6-mini-pro`), date snapshots, future variants, and identifiers with any other trailing modifier remain conservative unless separately registered. Physical capacity remains a separate lookup: GPT models in the wider mapped family may map to a 1,050,000-token physical window on API/OpenRouter routes (Astra's physical window is 1,050,000 tokens, with a 128,000-token max output), while the ChatGPT-subscription (Codex) lane is backend-capped at **919,000 tokens**, measured directly for both `gpt-6-astra` and `gpt-5.6-sol` on 2026-09-05 and lane-gated by the hooks through `DAAF_PROVIDER_SHIM` + `SHIM_BACKEND_MODE`. This lane-wide cap is measured, not provisional: the 2026-09-05 probes accepted 919,053 real input tokens on Astra and 910,827 on Sol while rejecting 922,552 and 921,973 respectively, which is consistent with Astra's documented 922,000-token maximum input (1,050,000 window − 128,000 max output). (An earlier 2026-07-16 Sol probe reported approximately 370,000 tokens; that figure is superseded and no longer describes the lane.) At the 919,000-token cap, the exact-Sol/Astra 60%/75%/90% percentage boundaries are 551.4k, 689.25k, and 827.1k tokens, respectively, so the 300k/400k/500k absolute gates fire first — the same ordering as on the full window. At a 1,050,000-token full window, the corresponding percentage boundaries are 630k, 787.5k, and 945k, so the unchanged 300k/400k/500k absolute gates likewise fire first and preserve full-window behavior. Likewise, `claude-opus-4-8[1m]` has a 1M-token physical window but remains conservative because physical capacity and quality-threshold profile are separate lookups. The same separation applies to **Opus 5**: `claude-opus-5` maps to a 1,000,000-token physical window in **both** its bare and `[1m]` forms — observed 2026-09-05 on Claude Code 2.1.261 via `/model` + `/context`, where bare `claude-opus-5` reported "42.8k/1m tokens" and `claude-opus-5[1m]` reported "48.2k/1m" — while its quality-threshold profile stays conservative-default per its Opus-class membership.

**Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`) is the one Opus model that departs from that pattern, and the contrast with Opus 5 is instructive.** The two models share an identical *physical* mapping: both map to a 1,000,000-token window, in both their bare and `[1m]` forms. They differ only in *profile*. Opus 5 remains conservative-default (40/60/75% OR 150/200/250k) because no DAAF quality validation promoted it. Opus 5.5 was admitted to the validated extended-horizon profile (30/40/50% OR 300/400/500k) on **2026-09-23 by an explicit user policy decision** (Checkpoint 1, `research/2026-09-23_FrameworkDev_ClaudeCode_Upgrade_2.1.280/SESSION_NOTES.md` § Key Decisions #3), which **deliberately departs from the 2026-07-24 "validate before promoting" doctrine**: no sustained-session DAAF measurement of Opus 5.5 output quality past 300k exists, and the promotion rests instead on the model's documented long-horizon design ("built for long-running agentic coding and knowledge work", `claude-opus-5-5` platform overview) and the user's acceptance of the risk. The provenance is recorded here, in both glob sites, and in CLAUDE.md so the departure is never mistaken for an evidence-backed registration of the Fable/Mythos kind.

The distinction is load-bearing in the glob spelling: the profile arm matches Opus 5.5 only as the exact terminal slug `claude-opus-5-5` / `claude-opus-5-5[1m]` (bare or behind `…/` provider prefixes), **never** by a substring such as `*opus-5*`. `*opus-5*` would substring-match `claude-opus-5` and silently promote every bare Opus 5 agent — a 250k reading would flip from CRITICAL to NOMINAL. `tests/bash/context_reporter.bats` and `tests/bash/subagent_bar.bats` each carry a pair of lock cases asserting that bare `claude-opus-5` and `claude-opus-5[1m]` still read CRITICAL at 250k. Note also the accepted cost of the promotion: on a 1M window the absolute legs still fire first, so HIGH arrives at 400k (40% of the window) rather than 200k.

Opus 5.5's own 1M physical window is registered explicitly (`*opus-5-5*`) alongside `*opus-5*` in the window arm even though `*opus-5*` already substring-matched it — an executed `case` repro on 2026-09-23 proved the implicit match — because that match is correct only by coincidence (Opus 5.5 happens to be 1M) and the next Opus generation may not be.

**GPT-6 Sol, GPT-6 Luna, and flagship `-pro` (2026-09-29 user policy decision, not validation).** On 2026-09-29 the user decided, by policy rather than DAAF quality validation (provenance: `research/2026-09-29_FrameworkDev_OpenRouterDottedModelNames`), that: (1) any **flagship** `-pro` model is read exactly as its non-pro base — same physical window, same 919k ChatGPT-lane cap eligibility, same quality profile — with `-pro` placed immediately before the optional `[1m]` (`gpt-6-sol-pro[1m]` accepted; `gpt-6-sol[1m]-pro` rejected), while mini/chat `-pro` spellings (`gpt-5.6-mini-pro`, `gpt-5.6-chat-pro`) are not flagships and stay conservative; (2) `gpt-6-sol` is treated identically to `gpt-5.6-sol` (1,050,000 window, 919k lane cap, GPT Sol/Astra extended-horizon profile); and (3) `gpt-6-luna` is treated identically to `gpt-5.6-luna` (1,050,000 window, 919k lane-cap eligibility, **conservative** profile). There is no `gpt-6-terra` (retired for the GPT-6 generation, per the user); it, bare `gpt-6`, GPT-6 mini/chat, and every other GPT-6 name stay unregistered (conservative profile; 200,000 window, except that an unregistered id carrying `[1m]`, such as `gpt-6-terra[1m]` or `gpt-6[1m]`, falls outside the GPT family pre-filter and reaches the generic `*\[1m\]*` arm, which maps it to 1,000,000 — the same pre-existing behavior as any other unregistered `[1m]` id, in the hook and the statusline alike). The resulting physical flagship grammar is `^(gpt-5\.(4|5|6)(-(sol|terra|luna))?|gpt-6-(astra|sol|luna))(-pro)?(\[1m\])?$`. The windows rest on the live OpenRouter catalogue fetched 2026-09-29 (`research/2026-09-29_FrameworkDev_OpenRouterDottedModelNames/web_fetches/2026-09-29T160403Z_openrouter-ai_untitled_051e0609.json`), which lists `context_length` 1050000 for `openai/gpt-6-sol`, `gpt-6-sol-pro`, `gpt-6-luna`, `gpt-6-luna-pro`, `gpt-6-astra-pro`, `gpt-5.6-{sol,terra,luna}-pro`, `gpt-5.5-pro`, and `gpt-5.4-pro`, and has no `gpt-6-terra` entry. The 919k lane cap for the new members is inherited by policy, not measured; the user's rationale is that capping can only make the reading more conservative (919k < 1.05M). **Caveat on `-pro` (retested 2026-09-29).** DAAFBench recorded on 2026-07-10 that the OpenRouter `-pro` slugs report roughly 4x the prompt tokens of their non-pro bases on byte-identical input, and that they hit a hard ~200k "Prompt is too long" ceiling on OpenRouter's Anthropic-compatible endpoint (`benchmarks/config/models.yaml`, "REMOVED 2026-07-10" block). A retest on 2026-09-29 (`research/2026-09-29_FrameworkDev_OpenRouterDottedModelNames/preliminary_notes/2026-09-29_openrouter-pro-retest.md`) no longer reproduces the ceiling. On `/api/v1/messages`, `gpt-6-sol-pro`, `gpt-5.6-sol-pro`, and `gpt-6-luna-pro` accepted 932,116, 942,594, and 931,924 reported tokens, with HTTP 200 at every step. `gpt-6-astra-pro` was only inflation-tested, so its ceiling is extrapolated, not observed. The inflation persists on both OpenRouter endpoints (observed): 4.11x for `gpt-5.6-sol-pro`, and about 3.12x for the GPT-6 `-pro` slugs. The hooks meter reported tokens. No wall was observed up to about 943k reported tokens (`gpt-6-astra-pro` untested), which is consistent with the catalogue's 1,050,000 `context_length`; that 1,050,000 is the actual ceiling is inference, not observation. On that basis the 1,050,000 window is kept for `-pro` in DAAF's own unit. The consequence (derived) is that the absolute gates fire at roughly 1/3 (GPT-6) or 1/4 (5.6) of the real content depth. For example, ELEVATED at 300k reported tokens is about 96k real tokens on `gpt-6-sol-pro`. That is conservative, not unsafe. Billing also follows reported tokens, so a GPT-6 `-pro` session costs roughly 3x its base for identical work (per-token prices are equal), and `gpt-5.6-sol-pro` costs more still, because its per-token price is 2x its base (about 8x combined with its 4.1x inflation). These cost multiples are derived from the token ratios and list prices, not invoice-verified. On 2026-09-29 the user decided to keep the 1.05M `-pro` window as built.

**GPT-6.1 Sol (2026-10-12 user policy decision, not validation).** On 2026-10-12 the user admitted `gpt-6.1-sol` on the same terms as `gpt-6-sol`: 1,050,000-token physical window, 919k ChatGPT-lane cap eligibility, and the GPT Sol/Astra extended-horizon profile, with the same optional `-pro` and then `[1m]` suffixes. It is one exact alternative, not a `gpt-6.1-*` wildcard: `gpt-6.1-luna`, `gpt-6.1-astra`, bare `gpt-6.1`, `gpt-6.1-sol-mini`/`-chat`, `gpt-6.10-sol`, and `gpt-6x1-sol` stay unregistered (conservative profile; 200,000 window, or 1,000,000 through the generic `*\[1m\]*` arm for an unregistered id carrying `[1m]` outside the family pre-filter, as above). The physical flagship grammar becomes `^(gpt-5\.(4|5|6)(-(sol|terra|luna))?|gpt-6-(astra|sol|luna)|gpt-6\.1-sol)(-pro)?(\[1m\])?$`, and `gpt-6.1-sol|gpt-6.1-sol[-\[]*` joins the family pre-filter glob. The 1,050,000 window and 128,000 max output rest on the OpenRouter `openai/gpt-6.1-sol` page fetched 2026-10-12 (`research/2026-09-06_FrameworkDev_GPT_Shim_Native_Search/web_fetches/opus_audit_2026-10-12/`). The ChatGPT-lane ceiling for `gpt-6.1-sol` was **measured on 2026-10-13** with `probe_context_ceiling.py --model gpt-6.1-sol` (shim 1.4.9, after the probe's cached-token fix). The lane accepted 919,744 real input tokens and rejected a request of about 921,973 tokens (a bisect target, not an exact count). That matches the Sol/Astra band, so the 919k lane cap applies unchanged. Run log: `research/2026-09-06_FrameworkDev_GPT_Shim_Native_Search/opus_audit_2026-10-12/scratch/ceiling_gpt-6.1-sol_2026-10-13.txt`. Deployment: `subagent-bar.sh`, `context-bar.sh` and the hook all carry the change. The hook got it through the user-applied patch `research/2026-09-06_FrameworkDev_GPT_Shim_Native_Search/opus_audit_2026-10-12/hook_patch/context-reporter_gpt-6.1-sol.patch`, which has been applied (commit 49b71dd; `context-reporter.sh` carries `gpt-6\.1-sol` in `GPT_FLAGSHIP_RE` and `GPT_EXTENDED_RE`), and the lockstep tests in `tests/bash/context_reporter.bats` pass.

**Provider-dotted Claude model IDs (2026-09-29).** OpenRouter reports Claude models with a *dotted* version (`anthropic/claude-opus-5.5`, `anthropic/claude-opus-4.8`, `claude-opus-5.5[1m]`), while every Claude pattern above expects Anthropic's hyphenated form (`claude-opus-5-5`). Before 2026-09-29 a dotted Opus 5.5 missed the anchored extended-horizon profile arm (250k read CRITICAL instead of NOMINAL) and a dotted Opus 4.8 missed the `*opus-4-8*` window arm (150k read 75% of a 200k window instead of 15% of 1M). Both the hook (once the user-applied patch `research/2026-09-29_FrameworkDev_OpenRouterDottedModelNames/context-reporter_dotted.patch` is in) and `subagent-bar.sh` pass every model ID through a byte-identical `normalize_model_id` function before classification. It rewrites **only** an ID that ends in `claude-<family>-<major>.<minor>`, optionally followed by `[1m]`, to the hyphenated form and keeps any provider prefix (`anthropic/claude-opus-5.5` becomes `anthropic/claude-opus-5-5`, which the anchored `*/claude-opus-5-5` arm already accepts). Every other ID passes through unchanged, so near misses such as `anthropic/claude-opus-5.5-mini` stay conservative, and GPT IDs are never touched. Normalization is **classification-only**. It drives the same/different-model comparison (both sides are normalized, because the session model cache may hold either the requested or the response spelling), the physical-window lookup, the ChatGPT-lane flagship predicate, and the profile selection. The shared model caches (`/tmp/claude-model-*`, `/tmp/claude-subagent-model-*`) and the agent-panel row label keep the RAW name. `context-bar.sh` does not normalize: it has no Claude window arm and applies no profile, and on OpenRouter it reads the window from the catalogue, where dotted IDs are native.

The window map and the profile selection are registered in `.claude/scripts/subagent-bar.sh` and `.claude/hooks/context-reporter.sh`, as two separate lookups. The profile arm and the pattern strings are identical across the two files; the window-map *functions* differ in code shape (an anchored ERE chain in `subagent-bar.sh`, a family glob plus EREs in the hook) but return the same window for every GPT flagship/mini/chat ID and every malformed flagship-family ID, such as `gpt-6-sol[1m]-pro` → 200,000 (one known, pre-existing exception: malformed `gpt-5`/`gpt-5.2`-family spellings such as `gpt-5-pro`, which map to 400k in the hook and `context-bar.sh` but not in `subagent-bar.sh`). The physical-window arm uses loose substrings: `*fable-5*|*mythos-5*|*opus-4-7*|*opus-4-8*|*opus-5-5*|*opus-5*|*\[1m\]*`. The extended-horizon profile arm is anchored for Opus 5.5: `*fable-5*|*mythos-5*|claude-opus-5-5|*/claude-opus-5-5|claude-opus-5-5\[1m\]|*/claude-opus-5-5\[1m\]`. The GPT Sol/Astra profile is the anchored ERE `GPT_EXTENDED_RE` / `gpt_extended_re`, and the physical flagship grammar is `GPT_FLAGSHIP_RE` / `gpt_flagship_re`; the latter, and the GPT flagship-family pre-filter glob, are also byte-identical in `.claude/scripts/context-bar.sh`. The full byte-identity lockstep tests (`normalize_model_id`, both EREs across all three scripts, and the family glob) and the reporter-vs-`subagent-bar.sh` window-agreement test live in `tests/bash/context_reporter.bats`. `tests/bash/subagent_bar.bats` locks only `gpt_flagship_re` and the family glob against `context-bar.sh`, plus a `subagent-bar.sh`-vs-`context-bar.sh` window-agreement test.

## Trigger Points by Threshold Profile

Reproduced here for self-containedness (this table is also maintained in
CLAUDE.md § Context Quality Curve). Percentage OR absolute tokens, whichever fires
first:

| Threshold Profile | Membership | ELEVATED at | HIGH at | CRITICAL at |
|-------------------|------------|-------------|---------|-------------|
| **Claude Fable/Mythos validated extended-horizon** | Registered Claude Fable/Mythos models, plus `claude-opus-5-5` (bare and `[1m]`) — admitted 2026-09-23 by user policy decision, not by DAAF quality validation | ≥ 30% or ≥ 300k tokens | ≥ 40% or ≥ 400k tokens | ≥ 50% or ≥ 500k tokens |
| **GPT Sol/Astra extended-horizon** | Exact terminal model slugs, bare or provider-prefixed, each optionally followed by `[1m]`: `gpt-5.6-sol` and `gpt-6-astra` (validated), plus `gpt-6-sol` and the `-pro` form of all three (e.g. `gpt-6-sol-pro[1m]`) — admitted 2026-09-29 by user policy decision, not by DAAF quality validation — and `gpt-6.1-sol` (with its `-pro` form) — admitted 2026-10-12 by user policy decision, not by DAAF quality validation | ≥ 60% or ≥ 300k tokens | ≥ 75% or ≥ 400k tokens | ≥ 90% or ≥ 500k tokens |
| **Conservative-default** | all Opus models except `claude-opus-5-5` (above), Sonnet, unknown model IDs, every other GPT variant, GLM models, and all other alternative-provider models unless individually validated and registered | ≥ 40% or ≥ 150k tokens | ≥ 60% or ≥ 200k tokens | ≥ 75% or ≥ 250k tokens |

The four severity **levels and their required actions are identical** across all
three profiles — only the trigger points differ. For the level definitions and
required actions (NOMINAL / ELEVATED / HIGH / CRITICAL), see CLAUDE.md § Context
Quality Curve.

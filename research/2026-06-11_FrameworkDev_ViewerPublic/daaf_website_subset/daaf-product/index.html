------
Regression Discontinuity: The Core Assumptions

RD exploits a known cutoff in a running variable (also called a “score” or “forcing variable”) that determines treatment assignment. The fundamental idea is that units just above and just below the cutoff are essentially identical except for their treatment status -- creating quasi-random variation near the threshold.

1. A Known, Rule-Based Cutoff

There must be a clear, predetermined threshold where treatment status changes. Examples: a test score cutoff for scholarship eligibility, an income threshold for program access, an age cutoff for policy eligibility.

The cutoff must be rule-based, not discretionary. If a committee decides who gets treated using the score as one input among many, you don't have an RD -- you have selection on observables (at best).

2. Continuity of Potential Outcomes at the Cutoff

This is the core identifying assumption: absent the treatment, outcomes would have evolved smoothly through the cutoff. In potential-outcomes notation, E[Y(0)|X=c] and E[Y(1)|X=c] must be continuous at cutoff c.

3. No Manipulation of the Running Variable (No Sorting)

Units cannot precisely control their score to land on a preferred side of the cutoff. The McCrary (2008) density test checks whether the distribution of the running variable is smooth at the cutoff.

4. Sharp vs. Fuzzy -- Know Which You Have
Even in short interactions, DAAF grounds itself in real citations and references to avoid relying on its fuzzy general knowledge or inconsistent results from web searches. Have more follow-ups? Want to write some actual code? DAAF is your flexible and responsive collaborator every step of the way.

------
Key Caveats

1. Title IV recipients only. All measures reflect FAFSA filers -- full-pay students (often higher-income) are excluded, so SES distributions skew lower than reality, especially at selective privates.
2. Coverage varies by institution type -- ~30-50% at selective privates vs. 80-95% at for-profits.
3. Home ZIP variables are ecological proxies, not individual/household measures.
4. Income tercile thresholds are fixed ($0-30K / $30-75K / $75K+), not inflation-adjusted across years.
5. Portal data ends at 2016 for student body datasets.

Confidence: HIGH -- sourced from the curated Scorecard skill. Year boundaries should be verified against actual data if precise coverage matters for your analysis.

What Becomes Possible #

A force-multiplying exoskeleton for your expertise

The goal of DAAF isn't to automate research writ large, and we firmly reject attempts to remove human researchers from the process. Instead, the goal is to remove the frictions and bottlenecks that prevent your hard-earned judgment and expertise from reaching further: in rigor, in scope, and in impact. When we harness the power and speed of Claude Code in a framework of responsibility, rigor, and reproducibility, what becomes possible?

Enhance your scope

Rapidly survey and integrate new data sources · Streamline collaborations with new coauthors and students · Implement new methodological toolsets · Generate complete reproducibility packages · Manage more complex data pipelines and workflows · Build interactive dashboards for disseminating research

Enhance your impact

Reach more audiences and stakeholders with summary websites and interactive explainers · Rapidly prototype new directions and frontiers for your research agenda · Respond to time-sensitive events and news with real evidence, sooner · Examine new geographies and contexts with lower barriers to data harmonization · Share new methodologies instantly with DAAF Skills

Public good infrastructure for the future of research

DAAF is built on a simple conviction: AI-empowered research tools should be a public good for the benefit of society. As an open-source project under the LGPL-3.0 license, DAAF will always be free. No premium tiers, no feature gates, no bait-and-switch, nothing to hide. My hope is that DAAF will only continue to get better as more researchers contribute, critique, extend, and build on top of DAAF to push the boundaries of what we can do with rigorous AI-assisted research, together.
